How it works

One run. It originates, kills, and only then values.

Spock runs a single gated pipeline on the company you give it. The order matters: it must have an idea worth arguing, and you must approve the assumptions, before any number is computed. Four of the stages are not Spock's to decide — they are yours.

A full run takes hours and pauses for you along the way. That is the design, not slowness — these are high-stakes decisions on our own money, and depth is the point. You can walk away between pauses; it waits.

  1. 01
    You
    You lock the scopeCheckpoint 1

    Scope-lock

    You point Spock at a company. It shows you exactly what it is about to look at — the entity, the countries it operates in, the lines of work it will pull — and waits for your nod before spending a minute.

  2. 02
    It originates

    The insight engine

    Forge writes bold reframes of the business, shielded from the current price so the idea is not anchored to it. Champion steelmans each one and pre-registers what would have to be true. Crucible then tries to kill them under an asymmetric burden. Most ideas are meant to die here.

  3. 03
    You
    You pick the thesisCheckpoint 2

    The taste board

    The survivors are laid out as options — always more than one — each with its wounds shown honestly, plus Spock's own recommendation. You choose the thesis the memo is built around. This one decision governs the whole run, so it is yours.

  4. 04
    It gathers evidence

    The specialist agents

    The evidence agents read in parallel — company history, unit economics, competitors, voice of customer, filings, red flags — each paired with an independent fact-checker that re-reads the same sources and tries to break the claim. In the same phase, a valuation architect designs the structure of the valuation.

  5. 05
    You
    You approve the assumptionsCheckpoint 3

    The lever sheet

    Before a single number is computed, Spock shows you the assumptions in plain English — how it is splitting the business, the discount it is using, the scenarios and haircuts. You approve them, or change them. Nothing is valued until you do.

  6. 06
    It values

    The valuation trio

    Only now does Spock compute worth — intrinsic (DCF), relative (peer multiples), and a synthesis that triangulates them against the deal price. The number that reaches you sits on the lever sheet you already signed.

  7. 07
    It red-teams and writes

    The AI-IC panel, then the memo

    A simulated investment committee of partner personas — each running on a different AI model, one mandated to oppose — attacks the drafted memo for what it got wrong. Their catches become conditions. Then Spock writes the whole thing up in plain language, and a chief auditor checks it before it is served.

  8. 08
    You
    You make the callCheckpoint 4

    The final call

    You get the memo and a clear verdict, framed to the actual decision — follow our rights, take a position, pass, wait. Spock's work informs; the call is yours.

Gold marks a human checkpoint — a decision only a partner makes.

The discipline around the AI

What makes the output partner-grade isn't the AI. It's the discipline that runs around it.

1

A fact-checker on every agent

Every agent ships its claims paired with a verifier that reads the same sources independently, with a different prompt, and tries to break them. No claim reaches the memo without a paired verdict — covered on Verification.

2

Sources carry their tier

Every claim travels with the tier of its source. T1 primary: audited statements, filings, commissioned diligence. T2 named secondary. T3 aggregators, for triangulation only. T4 unverified — flagged, never load-bearing.

3

It writes like the house talks

Lead with the plain business fact. Kill the noun-stacks, the acronym walls, the undefined jargon. Every sentence passes a read-aloud test. The memo is meant to be read once and acted on — a binding pass enforces it, it is not left to chance.

4

It learns, on the record

Every run ends with a debrief, and the lessons about judgement go into one growing register — with an honest status on whether each one is actually fixed in the machine yet. See the Learning Register.

What Spock does not do
