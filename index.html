Four moments are yours

Spock drives the run. It stops and asks you at four points — and never decides for you.

Everywhere in between, it works on its own. These four are where your judgement enters — each one a single, fast decision.

1Scope-lock

Before it starts, it shows you what it is about to look at — the company, the countries, the lines of work — and waits for your nod.

2The taste board

Once it has generated ideas and stress-tested them, it lays out the survivors as options — always more than one — plus its recommendation. You pick the one the memo is built around.

3The lever sheet

Before it computes a single number, it shows you the assumptions in plain English. You approve them or change them. Nothing is valued until you do.

4The final call

At the end you get the memo and the verdict. Follow, buy, pass, wait — the call is yours.

It originates

An idea, not a summary

A librarian only verifies. Spock's insight engine generates bold reframes of a business — then holds each to an asymmetric burden to survive. Most ideas are meant to die here. What survives is worth arguing.

It values

On assumptions you signed

No number is computed until you have approved the valuation's structure — how the business is split, the discount, the scenarios. The number that reaches you sits on a lever sheet you saw first.

It red-teams

A panel of rival models

When the memo is drafted, a simulated IC of partner personas — running on different AI models — attacks it for what it got wrong. Independent convergence is a signal; so is dispersion. Both reach you.

Why it is built this way

Diligence breaks at scale. A 500-page substrate has thousands of claims; a 90-minute room cannot catch every fabricated number, every silently-superseded projection, every confabulation between sources.

Spock does the rigorous, boring part well — so the partner room can spend its time on judgement, not fact-checking. It does not replace the room. It feeds it better-checked work.

The first objective is to learn

Every run ends with a debrief — not "what went wrong?" but "what did this teach us about how to think?" The lessons about judgement that each run mints are kept in one growing record, and only ever grow. It is the clearest proof that Spock improves.
