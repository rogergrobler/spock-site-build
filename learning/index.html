The Learning Register

How Spock learns to think.

Spock is a thinking machine, and this is its memory of how it learned to think. Facts about companies live in the evidence store; operational fixes live in the code history. What lives here is rarer: the lessons about judgement — how to reason about an investment decision — that each run has minted.

Continuous learning is the charter's first objective. This register is one growing record, and it only ever grows. Every lesson carries an honest status — a lesson that lives only in this register has not yet been learned.

Every entry answers four questions
1

What we observed

The concrete event — a partner's feedback, a failed run, a wrong number, a pack that missed something obvious.

2

What we learned

The lesson, stated plainly.

3

What level it abstracts to

Company, industry, methodology, pipeline, or craft. Abstract too low and you fix one company; too high and you get platitudes.

4

How we fixed it — if we did

The doctrine file, prompt, or commit where the lesson went to work — with an honest status. Roger approves every lesson, and its level, before it enters the register.

The insight — levels of abstraction

The whole discipline turns on one question: at what level is this lesson true?

A wrong number is a fact about one company. But the reason it was wrong is usually true of a whole industry, or of how the house values, or of the pipeline itself. Capture a lesson at the wrong altitude and it does not compound — abstract too low and you fix one company; too high and you get platitudes. Every lesson is placed on this ladder, and the level decides where in the machine the fix goes.

  1. 1Companya fact about one business — encoded in the evidence store, not this register.
  2. 2Industry / business modela reusable threat or dynamic every run in the sector inherits — encoded in the standing context layers.
  3. 3Methodologyhow the house values or judges — encoded in valuation and agent doctrine.
  4. 4Pipelinea missing or broken capability — encoded in an engine change.
  5. 5Crafthow the house writes and argues — encoded in house-voice and the templates.

The top rung — company — mostly does not belong here at all: a fact about one business lives in the evidence store. The register keeps the four that generalise.

LR-2

Accurate is not the same as valuable

Pipeline
Encoded
Observed

The corrected rerun was accurate — and mushy. No alpha. Nothing a partner could disagree with, and nothing a partner could act on.

Learned

Correctness is the floor, not the product. A research house that only verifies is a librarian. The pipeline needed an origination engine that generates bold reframes and then tries to kill them.

Fixed

The insight engine: Forge (bold, price-shielded ideas) → Champion (steelman + pre-registered contract) → Crucible (asymmetric kill burden).

LR-3

Never force an insight

Pipeline + methodology
Encoded
Observed

A run manufactured a reclassification where none existed — it framed a focused, well-run pet insurer as a failed something-else, because the doctrine could only represent edge as 'the market has the wrong category.'

Learned

Some companies are great exactly as they are. Quality-and-durability is a legitimate second edge; a forced reframe is worse than none, because it spends the house's credibility on a story. The engine must be allowed — required — to conclude 'no edge here.'

Fixed

The two-edge engine (reclassification and quality theses) plus the demotion discipline: wounded or unproven survivors are subordinate hypotheses, never the organising frame of a pack.

LR-5

Convergence is a signal

Methodology
Partially encoded
Observed

Three independent machines — the evidence pack, the AI-IC panel, and a fresh insight-engine run — arrived at the same base case by different routes.

Learned

Independent convergence is a calibration signal; dispersion is an uncertainty signal. Both are outputs, worth surfacing to the partner as such.

LR-6

A technical truth needs a human edition

Craft
Encoded
Observed

The v11 review memo was accurate and dense. Roger: 'seriously hard to read for a human.' A plain-English edition — same findings, examples instead of codes — landed immediately.

Learned

Two editions by design for substantial internal analysis: the technical edition (precise, file-and-line) and the plain edition a partner reads once. The machine may keep a version that works for the machine; the humans get a human one.

Fixed

Practised on the v11 review; to be written into the internal reporting convention.

LR-7

The output frame must match the decision the firm faces

Pipeline + methodology
Not fixed yet
Observed

The GoTyme pack framed the recommendation as 'take a starter position' — listed-equity, new-position language. But Chronos has held GoTyme since 2019 with a board seat, and the raise is a rights issue. The real decision is follow our rights, lean in beyond pro-rata, or don't follow.

Learned

The valuation was right and unchanged; only the decision frame was wrong — and a right number under the wrong frame is a wrong answer. The output frame must adapt to the actual decision: follow-rights for a rights issue, buy/sell for a listed line, follow-on for a private round.

Fixed

Patched for the run by hand. The durable fix is a first-class decision-context input (position · instrument · intent) captured at scope-lock, with the verdict branching on it. A v11 pipeline item.

LR-8

The valuation chart must triangulate methods, not plot the panel

Craft + pipeline
Partially encoded
Observed

The football field plotted the AI-IC panel on the price axis (an empty row — the personas give calls, not fair values) and showed only the blended house range — no DCF, no comps, no way to see how the methods triangulate.

Learned

Two rules. The AI-IC is judgement, not a valuation method — it never appears on a price axis. And a football field exists to triangulate the methods — DCF, comps, precedent, the round price — each as its own range against the blend. But only if the valuation agents emit clean per-method ranges.

Fixed

The render no longer plots the AI-IC and now draws a range bar per method. The per-method data still needs to be emitted by the valuation agents. A v11 pipeline item.

LR-9

QA must inspect the served artifact, not just the content

Pipeline — a missing capability
Not fixed yet
Observed

Two basic defects shipped in the GoTyme pack — the AI-IC plotted on the valuation chart, and a persona table whose Call column collapsed into an unreadable ribbon. Roger: 'that's pretty basic.'

Learned

Spock's whole QA stack inspects the decision object and the prose — facts, citations, coverage, language. Nothing looks at the rendered pack. So broken tables, empty charts, and a non-price plotted on a price axis pass unseen. Content-QA is not output-QA. A partner wincing at the pack is a failure.

Fixed

The two symptoms were hand-fixed. The capability is missing: a render/output-QA pass after assembly — deterministic structural checks plus a model render-critic that reads the rendered pack and blocks the serve until clean. A v11 capability.

The pattern library — seed

Lessons that generalise become hunting signatures.

When a lesson generalises into something screenable, it graduates here — where the standing learning agents can hunt with it across a partner-agreed watchlist.

Post-profitability jaw burst

A bank or insurer 0–4 quarters after first profitability, revenue growth far outrunning cost growth, the funding franchise over-delivering while credit deployment lags. The window where operating leverage is visible but not yet priced.

The hidden factory

An offshore IP or staff entity standing up country franchises on a repeatable chassis. The decisive test: do the inter-company agreements carry vendor economics, or only cost recharges?

Measured-delivery discount

A company raising against a plan, where its own plan-vs-actual history quantifies how much of the promise to buy.
