The agents

A research house, not a single model.

Spock is a cast of specialists, each scoped to one job and — where it makes a claim — paired with an independent fact-checker. They run in a deliberate order: originate the idea, gather the evidence, value the business, frame the decision, then red-team the lot.

The detail below is from the M-Kopa run.

1 · Originate

The insight engine

Before any evidence is gathered, three agents generate an idea worth arguing — and try to destroy it.

Forge

Writes bold reframes of the business, shielded from the current price so the idea is never anchored to it.

Champion

Steelmans each reframe and pre-registers what would have to be true for it to hold — before any evidence is gathered.

Crucible

Tries to kill each thesis under an asymmetric burden. Most ideas are meant to die here; what survives goes to the taste board.

2 · Gather evidence

The evidence agents

Each reads one slice of the diligence question and ships its claims paired with a verifier that re-reads the same sources and tries to break them.

CH

Company History

The audited paper trail.

Objective

Establish the audited factual baseline that every other agent's claims are checked against. Map regulatory and legal exposure exhaustively.

How it works
  1. 01Pull every available registry filing — Companies House, SEC, equivalents — across the full company history.
  2. 02Extract audited financials and unit-economic metrics year by year.
  3. 03Search court records and regulator databases for live and historical actions.
  4. 04Map patent and IP registrations per jurisdiction with expiry dates.
  5. 05Build a chronological timeline of board changes, secondary sales, and material corporate events.
Reads
Companies House · audited financials · court records · patent registry · board filings
Produces
Audited financial baseline · regulatory/legal exposure map · IP timeline
On M-Kopa

Built the complete eight-year Companies House filing history. Established the FY24 revenue baseline at $418m (goods + interest income, audited). Tracked three live court actions — Kenya High Court, Tax Tribunal, CMA complaint. Mapped the active patent expiring November 2031 across Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania — and a Nigeria gap that became a strategic IC question.

AI

AI Unit Economics

Does the AI claim show up in the financials?

Objective

Determine whether the AI thesis is visible in the audited line items, or only in management narrative. Grade the thesis on a scale anchored in evidence type.

How it works
  1. 01Build operating-margin and gross-margin trajectories directly from audited accounts.
  2. 02Decompose revenue and cost lines to isolate the AI-driven signature from other movements.
  3. 03Read cohort tables, retention curves, and unit-economic ladders where management discloses them.
  4. 04Look for headcount-efficiency improvements that should accompany a real AI deployment.
  5. 05Grade the thesis: A (cohort decomposition proven), B (real and measurable), C (claimed but not visible), D (marketing).
Reads
Audited financials · cohort tables · earnings transcripts · engineering blogs
Produces
AI thesis grade · audited financial signature · cohort decomposition read
On M-Kopa

Built the five-year operating-income trajectory showing 4.3× improvement to first-year positive group EBITDA. Decomposed FY24 revenue into $253m goods and $164.5m interest income — flagging the now-40% interest line as the underwriting flywheel made visible. Read the new-model loss-rate cohort at 2.2% versus 4.3% for the rules-based predecessor. Graded the thesis at B with a path to A via cohort evidence to request at the management session.

CA

Competitor Analysis

How wide is the moat?

Objective

Size the moat by mapping the named competitive set, quantifying market position, and identifying the specific erosion vectors with their lead times.

How it works
  1. 01Identify the named competitor set per market and per product line — not just the obvious players.
  2. 02Pull competitor financials and market-share data over multiple periods.
  3. 03Map share dynamics across the period to read the direction, not just the level.
  4. 04Categorise threats into three buckets: incumbents, AI-native entrants, hyperscalers.
  5. 05For each threat, identify the specific lever that would erode the thesis and estimate its lead time.
Reads
Competitor filings · press coverage · regulator data · commissioned DD reports
Produces
Competitive landscape map · moat-erosion timeline · head-to-head metric grid
On M-Kopa

Named seven competitors across four African markets. Quantified Kenya share dynamics — M-Kopa at 38%, next-largest at 12%, no other above 8%. Surfaced the Samsung Knox dependency as the single highest-impact moat-erosion vector and elevated it to an IC-blocking question for the management session.

VC

Voice of Customer

What do customers actually say?

Objective

Determine whether customer sentiment, retention, and brand strength corroborate management's growth narrative — or contradict it. Surface the pain-point clusters the diligence room would otherwise miss.

How it works
  1. 01Pull customer-facing review sources: app stores, Glassdoor, social signal.
  2. 02Read management's commissioned customer-experience studies on the same population.
  3. 03Build a temporal sentiment trend across multiple periods, not a single snapshot.
  4. 04Identify and cluster pain points from independent public signal.
  5. 05Compare against management's NPS / retention claims and flag divergences as structured questions.
Reads
App stores · Glassdoor · named-byline press · commissioned CX scorecards
Produces
Sentiment trend · pain-point clusters · churn / dispute signals
On M-Kopa

Triangulated four independent customer-sentiment sources across Kenya, Uganda and Nigeria. Built a nine-month NPS trend. Surfaced three named pain-point clusters — warranty service, lockout dispute resolution, repossession process — each carried into the management question list with its source citations.

PC

People & Culture

Does management have the bench to execute?

Objective

Determine whether the management team has the depth, continuity, and individual track records to execute the thesis. Identify key-person risk and structural gaps in the bench.

How it works
  1. 01Map the senior leadership and board from current filings and the management deck.
  2. 02Cross-reference against deal documents to surface silent demotions, new joiners, and quietly-departed names.
  3. 03Pull tenure, prior roles, and named public track record per individual.
  4. 04Benchmark bench depth role-by-role against the sector peer set.
  5. 05Read LinkedIn, named-byline press, and filings for departure-pattern signal.
Reads
Mgmt deck · 10-K bio sections · board minutes · LinkedIn · press history
Produces
Bench depth ladder · named track records · departure-rate flags
On M-Kopa

Mapped the full senior team against the Series F deal docs. Confirmed CEO Jesse Moore on his second decade-cycle at M-Kopa. Read the CFO's continuity into the fintech build. Bench depth across COO/CMO/CTO benchmarked against fintech-receivables peers — landed at adequate-with-watchlist for the management session.

RF

Red Flags

The things that kill deals 18 months after close.

Objective

Surface the boring-but-lethal items that don't appear in management's narrative — governance, forensic, regulatory, related-party — and route each to the partner committee with a source citation.

How it works
  1. 01Re-compute forensic ratios (Beneish, Dechow, Sloan) independently from the audited accounts.
  2. 02Assess auditor quality and audit-firm scale against the size of the group being signed off.
  3. 03Extract related-party transaction lines and trace counterparties where disclosed.
  4. 04Read governance filings for board independence and audit-committee composition.
  5. 05Map regulatory exposure not yet in management narrative — pending actions, tribunal decisions, complaints.
  6. 06Rank items by severity, attach the source citation, and route to the partner committee.
Reads
Audited financials · auditor reports · governance filings · regulatory actions
Produces
Binary closure-item list · forensic-ratio table · governance assessment
On M-Kopa

Re-computed Beneish, Dechow and Sloan from the audited accounts. Flagged the auditor — a small UK firm signing $418m group accounts — as a governance item. Surfaced the Kenya Tax Tribunal liability, a co-founder secondary with undisclosed counterparty, and three further binary closure items — each ranked, sourced, and routed to the partner committee.

3 · Value

The valuation team

Valuation is not one number from one model. An architect designs the structure, you approve it, and only then does a trio compute worth three independent ways and triangulate them.

Valuation Architect

Runs in the evidence phase

Designs the structure of the valuation before any number exists — how to split the business by revenue line, where execution risk lives, which scenarios to run, what discount basis applies. Its output is the lever sheet you approve at checkpoint three. Nothing is computed until you sign it.

Intrinsic — DCF

After the lever sheet

Discounts the cash flows the approved structure implies. Execution risk lives here in exactly one place — a measured delivery haircut drawn from the company's own plan-vs-actual record, not a guessed number — with any discount-rate anomaly disclosed rather than silently absorbed.

Relative — comps

After the lever sheet

Values the business against a named peer set on the multiples that fit its economics — separating a deposit or transaction line (which the market pays up for) from a lending line (which it discounts). The peer set and every multiple are shown, not asserted.

Synthesis

Triangulates

Reconciles intrinsic, relative, and the deal price into a single defensible range — and carries each method through as its own range so the football field can show how they triangulate, rather than collapsing to one blended figure.

4 · Frame the decision

Strategy

A right number under the wrong question is a wrong answer. Strategy turns the valuation into the decision the firm actually faces.

The strategy agent reads the whole evidence base and the valuation and writes the verdict — but framed to the real decision, not a generic buy/sell. For a listed line that is buy, hold, or sell. For a private round it is follow-on or pass. For a rights issue where the firm is already a shareholder it is follow our rights, lean in beyond pro-rata, or don't follow — with the option to syndicate the allocation.

It sizes the position, states the conditions that would change the call, and lays out the reasons not to invest as plainly as the reasons to. The output is a recommendation a partner can act on — or argue with — in one read.

5 · Red-team

The AI-IC panel

A simulated investment committee attacks the finished memo — before you ever see it.

Five partner personas, each running on a different AI model, with one mandated to oppose. They read the drafted memo and return calls, not prices — the panel is judgement, not a valuation method, so it never appears on the price axis. Its job is to find what the house missed at the point of conviction.

When the personas converge independently, that is a calibration signal; when they disperse, that is an uncertainty signal. Both reach you. On M-Kopa's sister run, three of five personas converged on a consolidation question the pack had filed under diligence — and were right to promote it to a binding condition.

6 · Write & check

The scribes, the Editor, the auditor

The memo is written in the house voice, then checked before it is served.

Scribes

Draft each section from the verified evidence and the valuation — plain business fact first, no noun-stacks, read-aloud tested.

Editor

A binding language pass — the house voice is enforced, not aspired to. Corporate jargon and undefined acronyms don't survive it.

Chief auditor

Runs the completeness audit — did every agent's finding actually land in the memo? — before the pack is served for your final call.
